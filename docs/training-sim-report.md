# Training simulator report

The real Toci engine against a fixed program (+2.5 kg every session), 56 days, 20 simulated athletes per scenario. Outcomes are measured on the virtual athlete (fitness-fatigue model), never by the engine itself. Generated by `python -m toci.simulator`.

Pass rules (fixed before the first run): no safety violations; fewer failed sets than the fixed program; no more workload spikes; at least 90% of its strength gain; never prescribes a lift the athlete can't do.

## Verdict by scenario

- **Consistent lifter: PASS**. The easy case: every session, normal life. The engine should progress at least as well as a fixed program.
- **No wearable (check-in only): PASS**. A consistent lifter with no watch or ring, which is every user today: only the morning check-in feeds readiness.
- **Missed workouts + a three-week break: FAIL** (failed: keeps the progress). About 1 in 4 sessions missed at random, then nothing at all in weeks 4-6 (vacation, illness).
- **Stalled near their ceiling: PASS**. An advanced lifter with almost no room left. Adding weight every week can only end in failed sets.
- **Two weeks of life stress: FAIL** (failed: no more spikes, keeps the progress). Weeks 3-4: bad sleep, low HRV, high stress. Readiness should catch it before the bar does.
- **Inconsistent availability: FAIL** (failed: keeps the progress). Only gets in on Mondays and Wednesdays, plus a random third day half the time.
- **Limited equipment (no squat rack): FAIL** (failed: only doable lifts). Trains at home without a rack. Squat and bench can't be done; everything else can.

## Limits

- The athlete is a textbook model with made-up but plausible parameters. It tests whether the engine reacts sensibly, not whether a real person gets stronger. Real proof needs planned-vs-completed tracking on real users.
- Workload spikes use the acute:chronic workload ratio, a common but debated injury-risk proxy.
- Runs are counted as load only; the run prescriptions aren't graded.
